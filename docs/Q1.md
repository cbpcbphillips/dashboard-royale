# Q1 — Which machines are truly underperforming once you account for exposure, availability and luck?

**Status: agreed plan of record (2026-11-08), built together by Carson and Claude (D-060 – D-079).** Replaces an earlier AI-drafted version (D-059).

Audience: a non-technical executive. Plain answers first; the math sits behind "How we worked this out" (D-044).

## The idea in one paragraph
For every machine, a model predicts what it **should** earn per open hour, using only the **other** machines: its spot and neighbours, machines running the **same game**, and the same days. The machine is then compared with that prediction. The biggest shortfalls are the truly underperforming machines.

## What we account for
| The question says | What it means here | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Exposure** | How long it was on the floor **and** how much foot traffic its spot gets (location is part of exposure, D-060) | Compare per open hour; the prediction knows the spot |
| **Availability** | How much of that time it actually worked | Only hours it was open count |
| **Luck** | Random swings in what it keeps | Hypothesis test (below); ordinary bad luck doesn't count against a machine |

## The prediction (D-061, D-062)
- **Leave-one-out**: each machine's prediction is learned without its own data, so it can't drag its own benchmark down.
- Inputs: its spot (bank, zone), its **game**, day of week, holidays and announced events.
- Compared within the same game, so a unit is judged against what that kind of machine earns in that kind of spot.
- **Same days (D-082):** each day's prediction is scaled by how the rest of the floor did against its predictions that day, so a slow season (the floor ran 6% below prediction in June) doesn't count against machines that were only there part of the time.

## The score and the ranking (D-063, D-064)
**Underperformance = (prediction − what it should have kept per open hour, luck removed) + its luck shortfall only if proven beyond chance.**
- If the luck test says "beyond chance", **all** of its luck shortfall counts.
- Otherwise its luck doesn't count.
- The page shows the **10 biggest underperformers**, with paging to the next 10; no arbitrary cutoff.

Why not rank on actual earnings: on this data, 102% of the shortfall of the top 10 by actual earnings was luck; 9 of the 10 were simply unlucky high-volatility games.

## The luck test (D-065, D-066)
- **Question it asks:** is this machine paying out exactly as its game is configured, so the gap between what it actually kept and what it should have kept is just chance?
- **Test:** one-sided z-test per machine; the standard error is exact (from every bet's size and the game's payout variance).
- **Many machines at once:** Holm correction, 5%, so the chance of even one false "beyond chance" call across all machines stays under 5%.
- **Beyond chance** → its whole luck shortfall counts, and it's labelled **"inspect payout"** (a lead to check, never an accusation).
- **Can't tell yet** → suspicious on its own (p < 5%) but not after correction; flagged with **"about N more months of similar play would settle it."**
- Today: beyond chance = **M-1158 only** (p = 0.000002; 0.0008 after correction). Can't tell yet = **25 machines**, needing roughly 4–21 more months.

## The play shortfall: is it real, and how big? (D-067, D-070)
- **Test:** same style as luck. H0 = it gets as much play as its spot and game predict; week-to-week variation gives the standard error; judged on a t-distribution with (weeks − 1) degrees of freedom, because a spread measured from few weeks is itself uncertain (D-082); Holm 5%.
  Labels: **proven below** · **can't tell yet** (with months of similar play needed) · **normal variation**.
  Today: 5 proven below, 33 suspicious on their own (~15 would be by chance).
- **Size:** machines really differ very little once spot and game are known (~1.4% typical), so each machine's raw gap is noisy. The ranking uses the **shrunk** gap: raw gap × the weight its own data deserves. Each row shows both.
  Example: M-1184 raw 12.6% below → shrunk 2.9% below ("proven below, most likely by ~3%").

## Labels: one tested classifier per factor (D-068)
| Factor | Test (Holm 5% across machines) | Labels |
|---|---|---|
| Play | machine vs its prediction, weekly variation | proven below · can't tell yet · normal variation |
| Luck / payout | exact z-test from bets × game variance | inspect payout · can't tell yet · normal luck |
| Availability | its downtime vs its maker's normal | proven extra downtime · can't tell yet · normal |
| Exposure | is its bank/zone significantly below the floor (+ days on floor) | proven quiet spot · new, only N days · normal |

## What the executive sees on each machine (D-071 – D-073)
Every proven label shows as a chip (a machine can have several; no single headline). The list stays ordered by the underperformance score.

| Label | Shown when | Plain meaning | Fix it points to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quiet spot** | exposure test proven | Few players come by this spot (including a game that does especially poorly in this area); the unit itself is fine | Signage, move the bank or the game |
| **Low sample size** | few days on the floor, or a test that looks bad but isn't proven | Detail line says which: "only 49 days on the floor" or "looks low; ~6 more months would confirm" | Wait and watch |
| **Down too often** | availability test proven | Broken more than is normal for its maker | Repairs, maintenance |
| **Paying too much: inspect** | luck test proven | Its losses are too large to be chance | Inspect ($1,200); a lead, not an accusation |
| **Unpopular unit** | play test proven | Gets less play than identical machines in the same kind of spot | Replace the unit |
| **Normal** | nothing proven | — | Nothing |

Because a game that does especially poorly in one area counts as part of the spot (D-072), units are compared with same-game units in the same zone where at least 3 others exist (otherwise game + spot effects). So the Jackpot Summit units in Back Forty are judged against other Jackpot Summit units in Back Forty, not labelled "unpopular unit" for a zone-wide pattern.

## The page (D-074 – D-077)
Top to bottom:
1. **Filters:** period (presets + custom), zone, bank. Short windows show a warning recommending a longer period.
2. **One-sentence answer**, generated from the data for the selected window (e.g. "5 machines get clearly less play than identical machines nearby; 1 is paying too much and should be inspected"). Never hard-coded; omitted if it can't be generated.
3. **Top 10 underperformers**, paging to the next 10. Each row: rank · machine · **$ short of prediction** (shrunk play shortfall + proven payout loss, for the selected period) · **label chips**.
4. **Floor map**: a dot at every slot, colored by **earning vs its prediction** by default (other metrics on a toggle), color-blind mode, values on hover.
5. **Machine card**: opens when a machine is clicked in the list or on the map; shareable `?machine=` link. Holds everything else: game, zone and bank, % below prediction (raw on hover), the four measures with ranks (expected win per open hour, coin-in per open hour, uptime & time occupied, actual vs expected win), each label's detail line, and "How we worked this out".

**Period changes recompute everything** (D-076): the leave-one-out model is fitted once and each machine's expected earnings are stored per day, so the ranking, dollars, all four tests and labels are recomputed exactly for the chosen days.

## How a judge can trust it (D-078)
What convinces: a **well-reasoned ranking**, an **explanation for every number and label**, and **claims a judge can look into and check**.
- Every row and card explains *why*: label detail lines and a "How we worked this out" section in plain words.
- Every number on the page links to its claim in the Evidence list, each with a re-runnable query.
- The "How we checked" tab holds the model checks, in plain words:
  - **Time split** (required by the judging rubric): learned from Jan–Apr, checked on May–Jun, compared with two simple guesses (zone × game average; the machine's own history).
  - **Does a machine's own effect carry over?** Split-half check (only partly: 0.16–0.25).
  - **Real moves:** M-1152 moved out of Back Forty; predicted 1.90×, actually 2.12×.
  - **New machines:** predict the first weeks of the 10 machines installed mid-period from their spot and game, compare with what they did.
